AI

AI lets an app's own Cloud Functions ask a language model to read, write, translate, summarise or sort, or ask a model to make a picture, speech, a sound or a video. The app declares what each use is; admins choose the providers and models, set the keys, cap the spending and see what every call cost.

What it is

PartWhereWhat it does
Settings, AIAdmin, Settings, AI (/admin/settings/ai)Four tabs: Setup (a Get started checklist, the mode, providers connected step by step, the models of each tier, model details and prices, the monthly budget), Purposes (each of the app's uses and its choices), Usage (the month's totals and a CSV of the calls) and Calls (the last 100, with what each one did)

Arc CMS has no AI use of its own. A site without an app that declares purposes has nothing to set up here.

Turn it on or off

AI is a switchable feature, id ai, on by default and needing no other feature. A site that has no use for it, such as a plain website, turns it off in src/custom/features.ts:

export const CUSTOM_FEATURES: FeatureChoice = {
    off: ['ai'],
};

On, it adds twelve small functions to every deploy, among them a daily cleanup job and checkAiJobs, which runs every minute and costs one Firestore read when no video is being made, and Settings, AI to the admin. Nothing is sent to a provider and nothing is spent until an admin connects one and switches to Live.

With it off, Settings, AI and its card are gone and the URL shows the not-found page; the functions setAiKey, removeAiKey, testAiConnection, aiSetupInfo, cleanUpAi, readAiFile, refreshAiBalance, aiVoices, aiVoiceSample, generateMediaImages, checkAiJobs and aiAvatars are not built (and the Media Manager has no Create with AI tab); and askAi answers off without throwing, so the same app code runs in a project with AI and one without. Records, totals and keys stay in Firestore. How the switch works for every feature: Feature ids.

Set it up

Settings, AI starts with a Get started checklist: connect a provider, choose models, check the monthly budget, switch to Live. Each step ticks itself when it is done. While the app declares no purposes and no provider is added, the page says instead that nothing on the site uses AI yet, so there is nothing to set up or pay for. A new site is in Test mode: nothing is sent and nothing is spent, and every call gets its purpose's sample answer.

  1. Click Connect a provider and choose one. Each says in a line what it is and where it processes data; OpenRouter, marked Easiest to start, is first. Google Vertex AI needs no key (see Gemini on Vertex AI).

  2. Follow the numbered steps the panel shows for that provider, with Open links to its pages: where to sign in, how to create a key, what the key starts with, and where to add credit. Alibaba Cloud also asks for its workspace ID; an other address asks for the address and where it processes data.

  3. Paste the key. The panel warns when a key does not start as that provider's keys do. Click Connect and test: the provider is saved on its own, the key goes to the server, and one tiny live call tests it, even in test mode. The test asks a short question, so Test model offers only the provider's text models: a model that makes speech, pictures, audio or video is tried by its first real use. It says how long the answer took and what it cost, or, in plain words, what to fix (a wrong key, no credit, a model the provider does not have) with the provider's own message under Details. The key is never shown again: the card shows •••• and its last 4 characters, who set it and when.

  4. Click Use these models, for a provider Arc CMS knows models of, to fill the tiers with its recommended models and save. Or list the models of each tier yourself: Cheap for tags and short texts, Fast for most uses, Thorough for hard reading. The first in a list answers; the next only when it is busy, down or gone.

  5. Under Advanced: what each model reads and costs, check what a model you typed Makes (text, data, pictures, speech, audio or video), what it reads (pictures, PDFs), whether the provider holds the answer to the schema or promises JSON only, what it Bills by and its prices: per million tokens in and out, with Cached input and Cache write where the provider bills them apart, or per picture, per 1,000 characters or per second. Arc CMS fills in what it knows, with the date it checked each provider's prices; a model without a price is counted as free, and a provider that reports each call's cost (OpenRouter) needs none.

  6. Check the Monthly budget (20 USD on a new site; 0 stops every live call). The checklist warns about each model in use that has no price, since the budget cannot count it. Switch the mode to Live and click Save. The page says when there are changes not saved yet, and warns when Live is chosen before a provider works.

Each provider card says how it stands: Working, Not working (with what to do), Not tested or Key needed. Replace key rotates a key the same way, with no deploy; it works within a minute. Edit changes its name or address (a new address or workspace means setting the key again), its Requests a minute and At once (empty uses the numbers shown, the provider's entry plan; set your plan's, or 0 for no limit), and for a provider that prices in another currency, such as Sarvam AI in rupees, USD for 1 INR: until it is set, that provider's prices count as 0. An other address also takes its Key header (Authorization: Bearer, api-key, x-api-key or another name); changing it means setting the key again. A provider whose key can read its balance (DeepSeek, what is left of an OpenRouter key's own limit, and the characters left of an ElevenLabs plan this month) shows it, read every day and on Refresh, with an optional Alert below: admins are told once when it falls under it (type AI balance low (admin)). Others link to their Billing page. When a provider says its account has no credit, admins are told at once (type AI credit gone (admin)), and the next model in each list answers meanwhile.

A provider marked Not tested live yet (Anthropic, the Gemini API, Mistral, Mistral EU, Groq, xAI, Sarvam AI, ElevenLabs, MiniMax, Kling and HeyGen) was built from its documentation and has not answered a real call; so were the speech and picture models of Google Cloud, OpenAI's picture models and Alibaba's Qwen-Image, marked so in the model lists. Its first real call is its Test, or for ElevenLabs and MiniMax, which have no Test, reading its Voices and its first speech call; if it fails in a way the message does not explain, report it.

A provider of presenter videos (HeyGen) has Avatars on its card: its avatars with their pictures, names and a search. A video purpose that presents names its avatars, and under Purposes you choose each one from that list, or type an id. Connecting HeyGen checks the key by reading its avatars, and Kling by reading its balance, both at no cost. Kling's balance is the units left of its resource packs, up to 12 hours late; HeyGen's is what is left of its API wallet.

A provider that speaks (ElevenLabs, Sarvam AI, MiniMax, and Google Cloud for its Text-to-Speech voices) has Voices on its card: its voice list, with a search, each voice's gender, languages and labels, and Play. Play uses the provider's own sample when it has one (ElevenLabs). Otherwise it makes a one-sentence sample in the voice's language, once: it costs a few characters, is recorded as Arc CMS's purpose Voice sample, and is kept a year, so playing it again is free. In test mode the sample is silent. ElevenLabs and MiniMax make no text in Arc CMS, so they have no Test: connecting one checks the key by reading its voices, which costs nothing. Sarvam AI has no voice list of its own, so Arc CMS shows the speakers its documentation lists. Google Cloud's voices need the Cloud Text-to-Speech API switched on in the project.

Below the tiers, Models that make files holds a list each for Picture models, Speech models, Audio models and Video models, shown when a provider has such a model or one of the app's purposes makes that kind. They work like tiers. Every kind has providers: ElevenLabs makes both sound effects and music for Audio models, and each audio purpose says which it wants, so the list may hold both. Use these models puts a provider's recommended speech and picture models in their lists, for the kinds the site uses: pictures always, since the Media Manager's Create with AI tab uses Picture models. Remove asks for a confirmation in the page, then removes the provider and its key from every model list and saves just that. Use these models also saves only the tiers and file lists: any other change on the page waits for Save.

Where a provider processes data is your choice and your client's: a provider's own service in China, for example, sends what you ask to China. OpenRouter and Hugging Face pass each call to the company that runs the model; block the ones you do not want in your account settings there. Read Gemini on Vertex AI for Google's regions.

Use it

Purposes

Each of the app's purposes shows its Kind (Text, Data, Pictures, Speech, Audio or Video) and what it takes and gives, with:

  • On: off makes every call for it answer off.
  • Models: the tier's list, or one of its own.
  • Daily allowance for each user or business (or what the app calls its keys, such as merchant), for a purpose whose app gives one: each one gets its own count. Empty uses the app's number. The app can give some their own number, such as from their plan, and that comes first.
  • For a purpose that makes files: where its files go (Arc CMS's private AI folder, which only admins open, or the app's own folder), Delete files after (days) for files in the AI folder, and Reuse files for the same input when the app gives it a cache: the same input gets the same file again with no new call and no cost. Switch it off to make every file afresh.
  • For a purpose that makes speech: Voices, each voice the app names (the first is its default) with the voice each provider uses for it, marked from the app or chosen here. Choose opens that provider's voice list, with Play and Use, or takes a voice id typed in. Use the app's takes your choice back. A provider with no voice chosen is skipped for that voice, so the next model in the list speaks. Your choices are saved with Save.
  • Keep what is sent and answered: off by default. On, each call's text, files and answer are kept with its record for 60 days and admins can read them on the Calls tab. They may hold personal data: turn it on while testing.

Arc CMS's own purposes are listed with an Arc CMS mark. Voice sample needs no models: it speaks with the voice you play. Switch it off to stop Play making samples. Media image makes the pictures of the Media Manager's Create with AI tab and Edit with AI: give it its own models, change each admin's pictures a day (20 to start) and the days before pictures never saved to the library are deleted (7), or switch it off to stop the tab making pictures.

Usage

For a month: the cost against the budget, the calls and failures, the tokens in and out (with how many were cached input), the test calls, how many were Served from cache (never counted as calls or cost), and the same by purpose, kind, model, user or business (the 20 that cost most) and day, with each model's amount in its unit (pictures, characters, seconds). Download calls gives a CSV of the calls kept, the last 60 days, the newest 20,000 at most; the page says when it held more. The cost is the provider's own figure when it reports one, else the amount times the price: an estimate, since the provider's invoice is the true figure.

Calls

The last 100 calls with the time, purpose, kind, outcome, model, tokens or amount, cost and how long it took; a call answered from the cache is marked From cache. A call that made files shows them under Files made: pictures inline, sound and video with a player, or Moved or deleted when the file is gone. Click one for each model tried and how it went, the size of what was sent, the key and reference, and, when the purpose keeps it, the instruction, the text, the files and the answer, loaded only then. A kept file opens in a new tab, from a link that works only on this page.

The budget

At 80% and at 100% of the month's budget, admins get a notification and an email (type AI budget (admin)). At 100% live calls answer limit until the month turns or the budget is raised. A month and a day are the site's, in the time zone of Settings, Localization.

Extend it

Purposes are the app's: declare them in functions/src/custom/ai.ts and call askAi from your functions (Use AI in your app). Any provider that speaks the OpenAI Chat Completions format works as an other address. A provider with its own format, streaming answers, conversations, tools the model may call and embeddings are changes to core, not to an app: raise them as feature requests.

Troubleshooting

You seeWhy, and what to do
Every answer is the same sampleTest mode. Switch the mode to Live and save.
A provider card says "Not working"It says what to do. Open Details for the provider's own message, fix it at the provider, and click Test again.
Calls answer off, "no model is set for the fast tier"Put a model in that tier, or in the purpose's own list.
"no model in the list reads pictures"Tick Pictures for a model that reads them under Model details, or add one that does.
"is at its limit of requests"The provider's limit a minute, or at once, set on its card, is reached and the call's time ran out waiting. Raise the limit to your plan's, or give the purpose a longer time.
"no credit left" (notification AI credit gone (admin))The provider says the account is empty; the next model in each list answers meanwhile. Add credit with the provider.
"Arc cannot make image with ... yet"That provider makes no files of this kind in Arc CMS, so it is skipped. Put a model of a provider that does in that kind's list (Picture, Speech, Audio or Video models). Test mode still answers with a stand-in.
Speech answers off, "has no voice for ..."No provider in the list has a voice for the voice the app asked for. Choose one under Purposes, Voices.
Speech answers off, a model "makes no mp3 at 32000 Hz"The app asks for a sampleRate that the models in the Speech list do not make in that format. Add a model that makes it (the rates are in Use AI in your app), or ask the app's developer to change the rate.
Speech answers too-largeThe text is longer than any speech model in the list takes in one request (Sarvam AI 2,500 characters, Google 5,000 bytes). Ask the developer to send a paragraph at a time, or add a model that takes more, such as ElevenLabs Flash.
"switch on the Cloud Text-to-Speech API"Google Cloud's speech needs that API on in the project; the Vertex AI API alone is not enough.
"has no key", "was set for another address" or "was set for another header" on a providerSet the key again. A key stops being sent when the provider's address, workspace or key header changes.
"the provider refused the key"The key is wrong, revoked or for another region. Paste a new one.
"Vertex AI refused the project's service account"Switch on the Vertex AI API and give the service account the Vertex AI User role (Gemini on Vertex AI).
"the model is not there"The provider retired the model, or it is not offered in that location. Put its successor in the list.
Calls answer limit with scope budgetThe month's budget is spent. Raise it, or wait for the month to turn.
Cost shows $0 for live calls, or the checklist warns "These models have no price"The model has no price, so the budget cannot count or stop it. Set its prices under Advanced, or use a provider that reports each call's cost (OpenRouter).
"The model used all its tokens thinking"A model that thinks first spent its allowance on thinking. Ask the developer to set thinking: 'low' or a larger maxOutputTokens on the purpose, or choose a model that thinks less.
