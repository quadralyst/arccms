Get found by search engines and AI

Arc CMS writes the technical part for you on every publish: structured data, a sitemap, robots.txt, llms.txt, feeds and a Markdown copy of each page. Your part is three settings screens and the way you write. The full reference is SEO and discoverability.

Fill in who you are

  1. Open Settings, then About. Set Site Name and Production URL, the address people will use, such as https://www.example.com.

  2. Under Identity for search engines and AI assistants, choose the Publisher type, and fill in Logo URL (absolute, at least 112 by 112 pixels), a one or two sentence Description, and your Profiles elsewhere (sameAs), one per line. These go on every page as schema.org data. Press Save Settings.

  3. Open Settings, then Discoverability. Every AI crawler is allowed by default. Block the Training bots if you do not want your pages used to train models; leave the Search and answer bots allowed, or assistants cannot cite you. Leave Publish /llms.txt and /llms-full.txt and Notify search engines on publish (IndexNow) on.

  4. Press Save and apply to site.

Canonical links and the sitemap are built from the Production URL. Left empty, they point at the Firebase Hosting address, and search engines index that instead of your domain. Set it before launch, then deploy the website so every page is written again.

Say what each content type is

Pages publish as an Article by default, which fits most writing. For a type that is something else, edit it under Content types and use the Structured data section: choose Product, Service, Event, Person, Recipe or How-to guide (or Blog post, News article), then map each property to one of your custom fields. Unmapped properties are left out.

Write each page to be found

In the editor, the SEO tab holds the SEO Title, the Meta Description, the Sources the page draws on, and Mark as updated today. The Checks tab scores the draft from 0 to 100. It is advice and never blocks publishing; 80 or more means the shape is right. It checks:

Habits that get pages quoted

Assistants quote passages, not pages, and Google rewards the same things:

  • Answer first. Open the page, and each section, with a complete answer in a few sentences; detail comes after.
  • Headings are questions people ask, in their words: "How much does it cost?" rather than "Pricing".
  • Facts, not adjectives. Numbers, dates, names and comparisons are quotable; praise is not.
  • Give it structure. Lists, tables, and the FAQ, Key takeaways, How-to steps and Definition blocks from the editor's grid icon, which also become structured data.
  • Say who and when. Name the author, and press Mark as updated today only after a real revision, never for a typo.
  • Cite and link. Add your sources, and link to at least three of your own pages.

Do not stuff keywords, rewrite what is already on the web, or write FAQs nobody asks. What only you can say (your data, your cases, your named opinion) is what gets cited.
