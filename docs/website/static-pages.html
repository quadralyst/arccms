Write static pages

Static pages are HTML files served at /p/{name}. Yours go in src/custom/site/pages/. Arc CMS ships two examples, a privacy policy and a cookie policy, in public/_site/pages/; put your own in their place before launch.

Write the legal pages

  1. Copy public/_site/pages/privacy-policy.html and public/_site/pages/cookie-policy.html to src/custom/site/pages/. They hold Arc CMS's own policies as an example; your copies replace them at the same addresses.

  2. Replace the <title>, the <meta name="description"> and the body text with yours. Keep the page a whole HTML document.

  3. Keep <arc-header></arc-header> at the top of <body> and <arc-footer></arc-footer> at the end: they become the site header and footer. Use the site's classes (Bootstrap is loaded) rather than your own stylesheet.

  4. Check them locally: with npm run dev running, open /p/privacy-policy and /p/cookie-policy.

  5. Deploy the website with npm run deploy, choosing Website or Everything (see Deploy). After the deploy it publishes both pages with your header, footer, styles and search box filled in, and lists them in the sitemap.

Link to a static page as /p/{name}. On the live site that address redirects to the published page at /pages/{name}; in npm run dev only /p/{name} exists.

The cookie banner (Settings, Site Usage) links to /p/cookie-policy unless you change it. The banner shows only on pages the app draws, such as sign-in, the form pages and search. Published pages (the home page, content pages and static pages) do not show it.

The terms notice on every signup form links to your pages by name: src/custom/site/pages/terms.html becomes the link on "Terms of Service" and src/custom/site/pages/privacy-policy.html the link on "Privacy Policy". Until you supply a page, its words show as plain text; Arc CMS's sample policy is never linked.

Other fixed pages: use a content type

For About, Contact and similar pages, create a content type, for example Company with the slug company, and write each page as an item. You get the editor, drafts, SEO fields, the sitemap, search and translations, and the page is live the moment you publish it: /company/about.

A page you add, such as src/custom/site/pages/terms.html, is published the same way at /p/terms on the next website deploy, and listed in the sitemap. Republish website (Content, Content types) also rebuilds every static page, for example after you change the header. Keep such pages for text that rarely changes and needs no editor; anything editors update belongs in a content type.

Static pages exist in the default language only. A page you need in several languages belongs in a content type.
