Collect sign-ups

A signup form turns visitors into contacts you can email: a waitlist, a newsletter or early access. Arc CMS verifies each email, sends a welcome email, can reward referrals, and files every sign-up in your audience. The full references are Signup forms and Audience.

1. Connect an email provider

Forms send a verification code and a welcome email, so do this first. Open Settings, then Email Settings, choose a provider (Gmail, SMTP or Resend), fill in the Sender Information, and use Send a Test Email. Until email works, the Signup Forms screen shows an "Email not configured" warning. For the emails you build in the Composer and broadcasts, set your logo, colors and footer in Brand Kit, under Email + SMS in the admin menu (Email when SMS is off) (see Email engine).

2. Set up the form

The first-run wizard already created a form named Waitlist, with the slug waitlist-form. Use it, or add one per campaign:

  1. Open Signup Forms in the admin menu. The page it opens is titled Waitlists: press Add Waitlist (or Edit on an existing form).

  2. Fill in the Name; the Slug is filled in from it, such as product-launch. The slug you create the form with becomes its id for good: in URLs and in your page's markup. Editing the slug later does not change the id, and a form on your home page that uses the new slug fails. Choose it once and keep it.

  3. Under Feeds lists, tick any lists everyone who signs up should also join (the form always has its own list). Once the form has tags of its own (its Tags item in the menu), Edit also offers a Default Tag for every new sign-up.

  4. Gamification (referrals & leaderboard) is on, so people move up by inviting others. Turn it off for a plain signup form.

  5. Leave it Active. When you close it, the message you set is shown over the form. Press Create.

3. Put the form on a page

Add the form to your home page, src/custom/site/home.html (see Build your home page). Any form with data-waitlist-form and an email field works, styled however you like:

<form data-waitlist-form data-waitlist-id="product-launch">
  <input type="text" name="name" placeholder="Your name">
  <input type="email" name="email" required placeholder="Your email">
  <input type="text" name="company" placeholder="Company">
  <button type="submit">Join the waitlist</button>
</form>
<p><span data-waitlist-count="product-launch"></span> people have joined</p>
MarkupWhat it does
data-waitlist-formArc CMS handles this form.
data-waitlist-id="…"The form's id: the slug you created it with. Optional: without it, the default form waitlist-form is used. On an older install whose default form has another id, that form is used instead. An id that matches no form creates a new, empty form on the first visit, so check the spelling.
name="email" or data-waitlist-emailThe email address. Required: without it the form shows "Email is required".
name="firstName", name="name" or data-waitlist-nameThe person's name. Optional: without it, the name is taken from the start of the email address.
name="source" or data-waitlist-sourceWhere the person came from. Optional: direct when missing. A hidden input works well here.
Any other field with a nameSent with the sign-up as a form answer, such as company above.
data-waitlist-count="…"Shows the number of sign-ups on that form. Left empty, it counts the form in the same <section>, else the first form on the page.
data-legal-noticeYour own terms notice, on any element inside the form (below).

Arc CMS takes over the submit: it asks for the emailed code, then shows the person's place in the queue and their referral link. On the home page this panel always shows both; the Gamification switch applies to the form's own page. While email is not set up (step 1), there is no code step: the person is signed up at once. These panels and their messages are in English on every page, translated pages included.

A visitor who arrives through a referral link (?ref= in the address) keeps that code in their browser for 30 days, so the referrer is credited even if they sign up on a later visit.

The terms notice

Arc CMS adds a terms notice above the form's button: "By signing up, you agree to our Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, and to receive emails from us." The two names link to your terms and privacy pages once you write them (see Write static pages). On a translated page it uses the legal_notice, legal_terms and legal_privacy keys of that language's strings file (see Publish in more than one language).

To write your own, put an element with data-legal-notice inside the form. Arc CMS then adds nothing:

<p data-legal-notice>By joining you accept our <a href="/p/terms">terms</a>.</p>

The full markup reference is in Signup forms.

Alternative: link to the ready-made form page at /waitlist/{form-slug}, for example from a button in an article or an email.

A data-waitlist-form form only works on the home page (and translated home pages). Pasted into a content item, a template or a static page, Arc CMS does not handle it, and nobody is signed up. Link to /waitlist/{form-slug} from those pages instead.

4. Edit the emails

Each form has its own group in the admin menu, under its name. Open Email Templates there and edit the OTP Verification email (the code) and the Welcome Email. Insert Placeholder adds merge tags such as ##NAME##, ##OTP## or ##REFERRAL_LINK## (see Email merge tags). These two emails are edited as they are and do not use the Brand Kit: put your logo and postal address in them yourself, and keep the unsubscribe link in the welcome email.

To keep writing to the people who signed up, use broadcasts and drip campaigns (see Email marketing).

5. Referrals and the leaderboard

Each person gets a referral code and link after signing up. Someone who signs up through the link credits the referrer, who moves up the leaderboard. The public ranking, with emails masked, is at /leaderboard/{form-slug}.

6. Find the people who signed up
